5.2.1 CNN Murmur Severity Model

The most immediate priority for the AI team is the completion and integration of the CNN Murmur Severity model. Currently, the XGBoost PCG model functions as a binary screening tool, classifying a recording simply as Normal or Murmur. The CNN model will cascade after this initial detection. When XGBoost flags a recording as a Murmur, the raw audio will be routed to the CNN to predict the murmur's timing (systolic vs. diastolic), grading severity (levels I through VI based on the Levine scale), and pitch characteristics (harsh, blowing, or musical). 

This requires training a deep 2D Convolutional Neural Network on high-resolution Mel-spectrograms. The architecture, currently in early prototyping, will utilize a modified ResNet-18 backbone pre-trained on ImageNet to extract complex time-frequency textures, fine-tuned on a heavily augmented subset of the CirCor dataset featuring cardiologist-annotated severity gradings. Once validated, this model will be integrated into the FastAPI `inference.py` pipeline as a conditional execution branch, enriching the dashboard's AI Results panel with detailed diagnostic nuance.

5.2.2 Hardware Acceleration for AI Inference

As the system scales to support concurrent monitoring of dozens of patients across multiple hospital wards, the CPU-bound YAMNet feature extraction step (currently taking ~1 second per inference) will become a critical bottleneck. The DevOps and AI teams plan to migrate the FastAPI inference service to GPU-accelerated hardware. 

The architecture will be updated to utilize NVIDIA T4 or RTX 3000-series Tensor Core GPUs. By compiling the YAMNet TensorFlow graph and the AuscultICor Keras models using NVIDIA TensorRT, the teams anticipate reducing the feature extraction and inference latency from 1.1 seconds down to under 50 milliseconds. This 20x latency reduction will allow a single inference container to process hundreds of concurrent MQTT streams in real-time, significantly lowering the per-patient computing cost and enabling true high-density ward monitoring.

5.2.3 Multi-Lead ECG Acquisition 

While the single-lead AD8232 provides excellent clarity for basic arrhythmia detection via the AuscultICor model, it cannot diagnose complex spatial cardiac pathologies such as myocardial infarction (heart attacks) or bundle branch blocks, which require a multi-dimensional view of the heart's electrical vector. 

The IoT team proposes upgrading the hardware platform to support multi-lead acquisition. The next prototype will replace the AD8232 with the Texas Instruments ADS1298, a fully integrated 8-channel, 24-bit analog-to-digital converter designed specifically for medical ECG applications. This will allow the ESP32 to simultaneously sample and stream Leads I, II, III, aVR, aVL, aVF, and the precordial leads V1-V6. The firmware's SPI communication routines will be rewritten to interface with the ADS1298, and the MQTT payload structure will be updated to interleave the multi-channel data. Concurrently, the AI team will modify the AuscultICor shared encoder to accept an `(N, 12)` input tensor, training the model on the full 12-lead PTB-XL database to detect a vastly expanded taxonomy of cardiac conditions.

5.2.4 Comprehensive Clinical Validation Study

Before AscultiCor can be considered for regulatory clearance or deployment beyond educational contexts, it must undergo a rigorous, multi-center clinical validation study. The project team has drafted a preliminary protocol for an observational, non-inferiority study involving 200 patients across three diverse clinical sites: a pediatric cardiology ward, a general adult practitioner clinic, and an outpatient geriatric center.

The study will compare AscultiCor's AI-generated cardiac risk scores and murmur detections against the gold-standard diagnoses provided by board-certified cardiologists using 12-lead ECGs and echocardiography. The primary endpoints will be the positive predictive value (PPV) and negative predictive value (NPV) of the AI flags in a real-world, uncurated patient population. This study will also evaluate the usability and ergonomic factors of the ESP32 hardware prototype, gathering critical feedback from nurses and physicians on electrode placement workflow, dashboard UX, and the clinical utility of the n8n-generated LLM summary reports.
